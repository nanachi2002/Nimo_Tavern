Most AI models need an API key from a provider that bills you. NimoLFM doesn't. It runs on Nimo's own hardware and you switch it on with one button. It's a small model, so it has real limits (read Limits before you start), but for everyday roleplay it's quick and costs nothing.

Quick start

  1. Open API Connections Click the plug icon.
  2. Find the NimoLFM card It sits at the top of the panel. The dot shows whether it's online and whether it's writing a reply right now.
  3. Press "Use NimoLFM" NimoTavern fills in the connection and settings for you and connects. When it says Profile applied and connected, you're done.
  4. Chat Pick a character and send a message as usual.

To go back to your own provider (OpenRouter, OpenAI, your own PC…), press Switch back to my own API on the same card. Your previous connection and settings are restored.

What it is

  • Model: Gemma 4 E4B by Google, in an uncensored community version (HauhauCS "Aggressive"), used under the Gemma Terms of Use. About 4 billion effective parameters, run in a compact 4-bit format (IQ4_XS) so it fits our GPU.
  • It thinks first. Before every reply it plans the scene: what you just did, how the character feels, what they do next. You can read its thinking in the thinking box above the reply; it is never mixed into the reply itself.
  • What the Nimo team did: the locked settings, the reply rules, the anti-repetition filter and the guards that stop it from writing for you (below), all tuned for roleplay in NimoTavern.
  • Reads long chats: up to 67,000 tokens of context.
  • Free for anyone using NimoTavern. It is not offered outside the app.

Privacy

We don't collect your prompts

Your messages go from your browser to NimoTavern's server, which passes them straight to the model and streams the reply back. We don't store them, we don't log them, and there is no telemetry or analytics on what you write.

Here is exactly what that means:

  • NimoTavern's server never writes message text anywhere. Its only memory of NimoLFM is short-lived counters in RAM used to enforce the fair-use limits.
  • The model server logs only timings and token counts, never text. To answer faster it keeps recent chats in RAM for a while; that cache is never written to disk and disappears when the server restarts.
  • The checker model runs on the same machine. It only sees the sentence being checked and the few before it, answers yes or no, and keeps nothing.
  • Cloudflare sits in front of NimoTavern, as it does for the whole site, so your traffic passes through their network encrypted in transit, like every other page.
  • Your chats stay end-to-end encrypted in your own account, as always. NimoLFM only ever sees the part of the chat that is sent for a reply.
  • Models can be wrong or strange. Don't put secrets in a chat you wouldn't want an AI to read.

Reply rules. On every message NimoTavern quietly adds a short instruction to the end of what you send (it never shows up in your chat): only the character acts and never you, react first to what you just said and did, then three paragraphs of about 150 words (first the action and scene in *asterisks*, then the character's speech in "quotes" with a small gesture, then what they feel or notice), with concrete detail and fresh wording, no emoji and no out-of-character notes. We tested many wordings on SillyTavern-style prompts and where to put them. At the end of your message they work far better than at the top of the prompt: replies went from about 30 words to about 150.

Anti-repetition. Small models tend to echo their own earlier replies as a chat gets long. NimoLFM runs with a repetition filter (the DRY sampler) that we set on the server. In our multi-turn tests it cut repeated phrases from 55% to under 10%, and replies that open with the same sentence from a third to none.

It never writes for you. Small models love to take over your character: making you speak, move or feel things you never wrote. NimoLFM stops that in three layers, all on Nimo's own hardware:

  1. Clear rules Every message tells the model which character is yours (every name you write under, even from older chats) and that it must react to what you just said and did.
  2. A name guard As the reply streams, every sentence is checked before it reaches your screen. A sentence that makes your character act or speak ("Haruka steps closer", "...," Haruka whispered) is thrown away, and the model rewrites from the last good sentence.
  3. A checker model Some sentences don't use your name ("I... I don't know," she stammered). For those, a second, tiny AI (a Qwen3-0.6B the Nimo team fine-tuned for this one job) reads each sentence and answers one question: is this the AI writing your character? It takes about 80 ms per sentence, so replies still stream. In our tests on scenes it had never seen, it caught about 85% of these sentences (around 80% when both characters are "she"), and wrongly flagged only about 1% of good ones.

When something gets thrown away, you only see the good part, so now and then a reply comes out a little shorter. If it ever still writes for you, swipe, and tell us in the feedback so we can teach the checker that case.

Your preset and card prompts are paused. A small model does badly with long prompts written for big models: jailbreaks, fake "thinking" rules, huge instruction lists. It can even repeat them back instead of answering. So while NimoLFM is on, NimoTavern uses its own NimoLFM preset, a fresh copy of SillyTavern's untouched built-in Default (even if you edited your own Default), clears Start Reply With, and ignores the system prompt and post-history instructions that character cards bring along (the card's description, personality, scenario and lorebook are still used). If a card relies on those instructions, move the important parts into its description.

The lock only applies while NimoLFM is on. Switch back to your own API and your own preset and settings are restored.

Limits

It's a small model

NimoLFM has about 4 billion effective parameters. Big paid models have many times more. Expect good scenes, not miracles.

  • Weaker at complicated stories. Tangled plots, many characters at once, long-term consistency, maths, code and obscure facts are all hard for it.
  • It can repeat itself, invent details or contradict the story. Swipe, edit the reply, or nudge it in your next message.
  • Mostly English. Other languages are hit and miss.
  • It writes fiction. Don't rely on it for facts, advice or anything serious.
  • Replies are capped at 2,048 tokens, thinking included.
  • One reply at a time. NimoLFM writes one reply at a time for everyone. When it's busy, your message waits in line, usually a few seconds. Fair use: up to 40 messages a minute and one reply at a time per account.
  • Best effort. NimoLFM runs on one machine run by the Nimo team. It can be offline for maintenance or restarts. When that happens the card says so.

Getting the best results

  • Write habits as rules, not descriptions. "She swears only by 'saints of salt'" is mostly ignored. "Use the phrase 'saints of salt' whenever she curses" works. Start with Always…, Never… or Use … whenever….
  • Plain facts are fine anywhere. Names, places, who knows whom: NimoLFM finds them in a long character card or lorebook, even far from the end.
  • Use World Info for big settings. Entries that only appear when their keywords come up keep the prompt short and put the right facts near your latest message, which is where this model pays most attention.
  • Keep important facts in the character card or the recent messages. The chat can be long, but a short, focused card helps a small model.
  • Prefer short, clear messages. Ask for one action or one reply at a time.
